# Chapter Two: The City to the Docks

Peter had to push the door in slightly to lock it. It never sat in the frame properly after the flood from the massage parlour on the floor above. The deadbolt made a reassuring clang and thud, but, out of habit, Peter still tried opening the door a few times.

He patted his pockets.

Wallet
Keys
Notepad

He rested his hand on his hip, considering the empty space there. *No. Guns just make trouble. Umbrella should be enough.* He popped another mint and headed down the dim hallway to the stairwell. On his way down to the street level he bitterly thought of his old plans to get out of this dump and into a proper office. One in a building with a working elevator and cleaners. It would have all been possible if he had just kept his streak of high profile cases going.

The puddle by the street exit door didn't bode well. Stepping out onto the street, he was hit by a wind he did not expect. Swivelling to face the gust, he gripped his umbrella tightly and opened it. His side had only been half soaked by the rain before he was safely under cover.

*Could have been worse,* he shrugged.

The streetlights seemed to burn brighter through the rain now that the sun had set. He checked his watch. 7:00pm. *I could make it to the docks by 7:30 if I huff it.*

Despite the wind, rain, and darkness, people were out. Skyscrapers towered over the cab-filled streets below, and the sidewalks were a shifting fabric sheet of umbrellas.

Peter waded through them, going against the current. Like the tide, workers rolled into the city in the morning, and rolled out at the end of the work day. This being a Friday, the tide of people was delayed by after-work cocktails.

To his surprise, the rain stopped. He realized it had actually stopped a few minutes ago, but he, like those around him, was so conditioned by the city's curse of rain that it didn't seem possible to be outside without the cover of an umbrella. He lowered his, expecting to be greeted with a view of the long avenue that lead to the docks. Instead, he couldn't see farther than a dozen meters.

He laughed to himself, "It figures."

The towering skyscrapers were murky behemoths. The streetlights dampened, as if under a layer of gray cotton - a fog had rolled in. Thicker than any he had seen before, and it almost seemed as if he was getting wetter walking through it than if it had been raining. It pooled in, swirling around street corners and billowing up. It carried with it the strong smell of the ocean. Peter kept walking. Heading south to the docks.

The dense urbanity of the downtown core gave way to plain, industrial buildings and chain link fences. The slim shapes in suits and dresses, topped with umbrellas, were traded for bulky figures draped in heavy rain jackets. He was getting close now. The union headquarters was right on the water, and he was running out of road. In the final block, even the workers started to thin out. The only people to be seen around the union building were still burly like the working men, but were distinct in their more casual clothing, with slick backed hair. Union men. Enforcers. Closer to gang members, really. Peter could tell which ones had done time at a glance. He couldn't help but picture his brother at their mercy. The stories he's heard about fingers cut off or teeth knocked out made him shutter. Each one he passed watched him closely.

In the final block leading up to the union building, the fog somehow got even thicker. The street behind him blurred into an opaque slate. Peter felt as if his exit was closing in behind him, that there would be no going back.

As he approached the main door a gruff voice barked at him. "Hey fella! Over here. The boss is waitin' for yah." Clearly this man was a lookout, as he didn't leave his post. Instead, yet another burly figure peaking out from the shadows on the side of the building beckoned him. 

Peter rounded the corner and had to speed up to walk quickly to catch up to the man, who was well on his way to the back of the building. The smell of the sea was overpowering now. The damp fog made him feel as if he was swimming. Rounding the final corner, he saw the man standing by a dimly lit doorway. He held the door open. It was a black, nothingness inside. Peter took a breath and stepped into the warehouse.
