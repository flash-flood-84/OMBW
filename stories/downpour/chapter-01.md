Rain battered the windows of the shabby little office. The cold gray light of the wet weather mixed with the orange hued street lights and seeped through the blinds, falling in lines across the desk. Peter looked up from the case file before him to check the time. 6pm. He let out a long sigh, closed the file and rolled his old office chair over to the filing cabinet.

"Fuhhhhhhhhhh..." he opened the cabinet and fingered through to the back.

"...uhhhhhhhhhhh..." Finally, he put the case file in the "Cold Storage" folder.

"...uhhhck!" He wheeled back to his desk and popped a mint in his mouth. "That's fine. IT'S FINE. Fine, fine."

It was the third case in a row that he'd struck out on. Looking up at the wall, he realized, with disgust, that he would have to start taking infidelity cases again. His gaze was set on a framed newspaper front page. "Local P.I. puts mayor behind bars." He really thought that was the start of his big break. But now he had the sinking feeling that it was his peak, with nowhere to go but down.

The cases after that, with his new renown, brought in famous and powerful clients, and big paychecks. But, now that he had come up empty handed more often, that stream of high paying clients had dried up. He could see that surveiling real estate agents, whose husbands swore they could smell another man's cologne, was in his future. "I guess it pays the rent," he said to the off-white walls.

This last one would have been good though. Jimmy, the owner of a large construction company, suspected the co-owner, his cousin, was using company funds to finance his failing pizza place. Peter messed up and spooked the cousin - he was spotted following him one night. After that, the cousin cleaned up enough evidence that the trail went cold, and Peter had nothing left to work with. But what's worse is the cousin went straight to Peter's client and the two ended up in a fist fight. It goes without saying that there was no pay day for Peter.

The rain's assault on the windows continued. Louder now than before, it reminded Peter of frantic knocking on a door. 

*Wait what? Oh my god it's a client. Don't screw this up Peter.* 

He quieted his thoughts. Trying not to sound desperate, he cleared his throat and managed a reasonably calm "Come in!"

In walked a short, soggy figure. A man in a drenched, black (or was it gray when dry?) suit. The man paused to wipe rain droplets off of his glasses. It took Peter a minute to recognize him.

"Fuck off! Out! Out now! I told you I never want to see your face again! Actually wait. Where's my god damn money?"
