# Chapter One: The Office

Rain battered the windows of the shabby little office. The cold gray light of the wet weather mixed with the orange-hued streetlights and seeped through the blinds, falling in lines across the desk. Peter looked up from the case file before him to check the time. 6:00 pm. He let out a long sigh, closed the file and rolled his old office chair over to the filing cabinet.

"Fuhhhhhhhhhh..." He opened the cabinet and fingered through to the back.

"...uhhhhhhhhhhh..." Finally, he put the case file in the "Cold Storage" folder.

"...uhhhck!" He wheeled back to his desk and popped a mint in his mouth. "That's fine. IT'S FINE. Fine, fine."

It was the third case in a row that he'd struck out on. Looking up at the wall, he realized, with disgust, that he would have to start taking infidelity cases again. His gaze was set on a framed newspaper front page. "Local P.I. puts mayor behind bars." He really thought that was the start of his big break. But now he had the sinking feeling that it was his peak, with nowhere to go but down.

The cases after that, with his new renown, brought in famous and powerful clients, and big paychecks. But, now that he had come up empty-handed more often, that stream of high-paying clients had dried up. He could see that surveilling real estate agents, whose husbands swore they could smell another man's cologne, was in his future. "I guess it pays the rent," he said to the off-white walls.

This last one would have been good, though. Jimmy, the owner of a large construction company, suspected the co-owner, his cousin, was using company funds to finance his failing pizza place. Peter messed up and spooked the cousin - he was spotted following him one night. After that, the cousin cleaned up enough evidence that the trail went cold, and Peter had nothing left to work with. But what's worse is that the cousin went straight to Peter's client and the two ended up in a fist fight. It goes without saying that there was no payday for Peter.

The rain's assault on the windows continued. Louder now than before, it reminded Peter of frantic knocking on a door. 

*Wait, what? Oh my god it's a client. Don't screw this up, Peter.* 

He quieted his thoughts. Trying not to sound desperate, he cleared his throat and managed a reasonably calm, "Come in!"

In walked a short, soggy figure. A man in a drenched, black (or was it gray when dry?) suit. The man paused to wipe rain droplets off of his glasses. It took Peter a minute to recognize him.

"Fuck off! Out! Out now! I told you I never wanted to see your face again! Actually wait. Where's my god damn money?"

The man took a step back and put out his hands, as if ready for physical defense. "Pete, wait!" The soggy man pleaded. "Calm down. Hey!"

The man dodged a flying eraser.

"Hey, it wasn't my fault! You and I were both screwed over. I thought you understood that. Stop!"

The man dodged a flying stapler.

"It's John! He's in trouble!"

The projectiles stopped.

Peter clenched his jaw. Rising out of his angrily hunched shoulders to his full height, his eyes bored a hole into the man. Slowly, he said, "Don't mess with me, Stu. If you're using that name just to get something from me, I'll grab that stapler and..."

Stu begged, "No, Pete. I swear. John needs help. He needs your help!"

"If my brother got himself in a mess, then he can dig his own stupid self out of it."

Stu looked down, noticing the droplets falling from his suit onto the wood floors. A small puddle was beginning to form.

Peter looked at the puddle too, then back up at Stu. The two men broke into laughter.

Peter's eyes softened. "Pull up a chair." He motioned to the 2 folding chairs leaning on the wall.

Stu said nervously, "You sure? I'm drenched."

Peter nodded and pointed to the mop next to the chairs. "In this town? You think  you're the first person to burst in here dripping wet? Hey, where's your umbrella anyway?"

"The forecast called for sun."

"God, they need to fire that guy. What's his network name? Timmy Lightning?"

Stu plopped down on the chair. He winced through the pain in his knee. "Agh. No, I'm alright it's fine... Johnny Thunders. Stupid name, I know."

Peter sat back in his chair, which gave out a little creak. He motioned to a bowl on his desk. 

"Mint?"

"You're still off smokes?"

"Ya. Mint?"

Stu grabbed a mint and popped it in his mouth. It tinkled around his teeth and palate as he said, "So can we talk about your brother?"

Peter let out a long sigh. "Ugh. Fine."

Stu unbuttoned his coat and cleared his throat. "It's the dockworkers' union he pissed off this time. He swears it's not his fault. He got mixed up with their accountant, Artie Summers, small man, unusually stupid for a number guy. John somehow convinced him to place a bet at the racetrack."

Peter popped a mint.

"I don't know all the details, but the wrong horse won, the accountant is dead, and the union is after John for the money. Summers used union money to place the bet."

"He'd better just leave town like usual," Peter casually suggested.

Stu squinted. "It's too late. They've got him."

Peter muttered under his breath while shaking his head, "John, you idiot."

Stu looked away nervously. The newspaper clippings on the wall caught his eye. His eyes glistened ever so slightly. "He was so proud of you. His baby brother making the front page - making a difference. All he ever made in his miserable life was trouble."

Peter's eyebrows raised.

Stu quickly added, "His words, not mine." He adjusted his collar. "Anyways, I had to come here and plead his case, Pete. Here's the address." Stu handed over a napkin with his chicken scratch on it. "I think you still have time to get the union to come to an understanding." He looked at his watch. "Oh shit! Hey I gotta run. Call me if you..." He stopped short. "Actually, I can't be involved in this any more than I already am. Tell your brother he owes me for getting you."

Peter rolled his eyes. As Stu walked out of his office, he reviewed his arsenal of projectiles. Nothing jumped out at him so the soggy man left without injury. He looked down at the napkin. *2301 Wayside Drive.* 

"Shit," he whispered, and the rain continued.
