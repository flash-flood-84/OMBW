# Chapter Three: The Warehouse

The P.I. blinked rapidly, trying to get his eyes to adjust to the dark interior. Eventually, he was able to make out a string of lights and large boxey shaped. He ventured further into the warehouse and could see five rows of shipping containers. Each container had its own little lamp, giving the impression of a straight line of little fires.

Near the middle, he saw an open container with light pouring our of it. The warm light of the container's lamp was contrasted with a cold, clinical, florescent light radiating from the inside.

A man, vaguely fitting into the same model as the others, called out. "Hey boss. He's here!"

Peter stepped in line with the door to the container. Again, his eyes had to adjust. A man in a chair. No. Two men. One of them now rising up out of his chair and the other sitting still. A puddle under the unmoving man. Red.

"John!" Peter exclaimed. "What did they do to you?"

The scene came into full focus now. John was slumped over in the chair, his node bloody and his hand bandaged. The other man, small and smiling, walked toward Peter. Suddenly a gun was pressed into Peter's lower back.

"Petah, Petah, Petah. I've heard so much about ya." The man spoke in a quiet rasp. It was like dry cement being poured out of a bag. "According to your brother, you're just the man I need." The man was all smiles still. One gold tooth flashing, his hands held out and open, he wore a silk shirt, italian shoes, and a gold watch. He came to a stop and rested a hand on John's shoulder.

Peter didn't budge. He stood there, waiting.

"Alright, alright. I'm sorry. So rude of me to put you at a disadvantage." The man chuckled - more cement pouring out. "Vince Gambaldi, president of the Dockworkers' union, local chaptah 402." He bowed his head towards Peter.

Peter's eyes shifted to his brother.

"Oh don't worry. He's fine. He's alive. He's just takin' a little nap. See?"

Vince poked the reddest part of John's bandaged hand. John's head shot up and he howled in pain, before passing out again.

Peter looked into Vince's brown eyes. "How much?"

"Oh Peter," Vince said with a chuckle. He paused and suddenly the laughter stopped, and, for the first time, Vince's face lost its smile and became a serious. "My friend. This ain't about money no more."

Vince sat and motioned to an empty chair. Peter stayed where he was.

*CRACK.* He felt the butt of the gun slam into the back of his head. He stumbled and caught himself on the wall.

"When the boss says sit, you sit," intoned the hulking enforcer.

"Thank you Tony." Vince's smile returned and his gold tooth glinted again.

Peter sat down, rubbing the back of his head. The pressure from the tip of the gun now rested between his shoulder blades. He leveled his eyes with Vince's. "Are you sure this can't be about money?"

Vince's gaze drifted to Peter's brother, and he said with a lightness, "You know, Peter, when we got our hands on John we knew he couldn't pay the money. Sure, it was our guy who made that but, but Peter," He paused, the smile leaving his face again. "Sometimes I get a bit irrational."

Up until this point Peter had remained relatively calm. He's seen his share of crazy shit. He's had guns pointed at him before. But something in Vince's face got to him. Or was it a lack of something? Whatever it was sent a chill down his spine. He wasn't sitting with a man in a shipping container. This was a mountain lion in a cave.
