# Chapter Four: The Break-in

The rain returned as Peter walked back into town. The fog cleared out as well. The glowing figures of the union men and workers that peered out through the fog were now looming shadows in a dark night. The thugs had umbrellas, but Peter noticed an odd thickness to their shafts. He couldn't be sure in the darkness, but the tips appeared more pointed than those of normal umbrellas. Either way, these men glared at him ominously on his way in, but now he felt almost invisible to them. Word must have gotten out. *Am I under protection now? From what?*

He knew he needed to hurry, but his office was on the way to The Big Squeeze and there were a few things he would need. The southernmost part of the city was the docks - the industrial outskirts. Then just above that was the shabby part of the city. Of course, that's where Peter's crappy little office was. The Big Squeeze was right at the heart of the city. Peter had been there one time before. Well, he tried to get in, anyway.

It was when times were still good and he was on a case for a high-paying, high-profile client. He actually couldn't believe it. It was like out of some movie. The director of the city's museum of fine arts contacted him directly in order to track down a stolen painting. He thought he had it all figured out, but he just needed one more piece of information from someone - the chief curator's mistress.

Peter knew she frequented The Big Squeeze, but when he tried to get in one night the doormen refused him. 

"No cops," one of them said in a deep voice.

"Hey buddy, I'm just here for a good time," Peter lied.

"Well how come I seen yah in the paper with a bunch of cops?"

Peter stood there a moment, thinking.

The other doorman cracked his knuckles and turned to Peter. "You need some help understanding, buddy?"

Peter held his hands up. "Fellas, fellas. I'm sure this will all make sense after you talk to my friend. He'll clear all this up." He held up a bill with a notable president on it. "Maybe you know him?"

The two doormen nodded at each other and, quick as a flash, one grabbed the money from Peter's outstretched hand while the other grabbed Peter. The next thing he knew, he was waking up in a hospital bed. Peter liked to believe that the man didn't see the car coming when he threw him into the street, but that was probably wrong. Of course, there were no witnesses. Without that final piece of evidence, he was never able to prove out his theory in the case and his client fired him. What made the memory all the more bitter was that it was the beginning of his cold streak. 

A thought popped into his head now. *Those thugs weren't taking any chances. Even a plain clothes P.I. couldn't get in. And now the head of the Dockworkers' union is sending me to meet an associate there? This place must be more than your average social club.* He considered the gun in his office. *I'll decide when I'm there. Maybe the feel of it in my hand will tell me to bring it or not.*

Ascending the stairs to his office on the second floor, he was struck by a particular smell. Cigarette smoke. No. Cigar. *No one in this crappy old building can afford cigars. Who could that have been?*

His eyes widened as he entered the hallway to his office. Even from the other end of the hall, he could tell something had happened to the door. Quickly closing the distance, he saw chunks taken out of the frame and the deadbolt casing was blown out, as if the door had been forced with a crowbar. Amidst his immediate shock, and rising adrenaline with the prospect of dealing with a potential intruder, he still had the strong thought, *Crowbar? There's easier ways to break in. I know. I've had to pick the lock a few times when I forgot my keys.*

Now, wishing he had brought his gun after all, he clutched his umbrella tightly. Sure, his wasn't like those of the union men, but it had a solid ash shaft and stainless steel frame. 

Note to reader: Since it is always raining in this city, everyone has an umbrella. Umbrella's have been elevated to a required item in this world, and many people form emotional bonds with them, as you would with a particularly good pair of shoes or pants. Maybe a car would be a better comparison. End note.

He held his breath, listening. It was 9:00 pm now. The whole building was silent, including his office. He exhaled and opened the door. *THUD.* The door got about 45 degrees open and suddenly stopped. Peter tried forcing it, and it gave by a few inches, but no more. Still tense, he cautiously peered around the door to see what was blocking it. Halfway into the room now, he leapt in horror away from the door and to the opposite side of the office. He held the pointed end of his umbrella at the motionless man on the ground.

"HEY!" Peter yelled. Open to the idea of a fight, he puffed out his chest and yelled again. "Hey you sonovabitch! What are you doing in my office?"

The man didn't move. The man wasn't breathing. Peter edged closer and saw a pool of blood under the man's head.
