"Jim, just put that anywhere. We can figure it all out eventually."

He muttered, "Mom, not everything can just be figured out later."

"What's that, dear?"

"I said what do you want for dinner later? I'm buying."

"Oh, don't be silly. I'll whip up a casserole."

Jim shook his head and smiled. "No, YOU don't be silly. You're letting me stay here till I get back on my feet. The least I can do is buy your dinner tonight."

His mother put down the moving box she was holding and crossed her arms. "Fine, but I get to pick the place!"

Sandra, Jim's mother, lived in a small bungalow which was a few blocks from the main strip. It was a little run-down and it didn't have any view of the water, but she owned it outright and was proud. The front door spilled right into the living room, which had a dozen boxes piled up.

"I don't know how you lived in Vancouver with so little. Where's your stuff? And no furniture?"

"That's probably one of the reasons Supatec went under, mMm. They were spending like crazy. Fully furnished apartments. Not like I was home often, though. We were pulling all-nighters all the time. I basically lived at the office."

His mother fiddled with her ring. "Well, hopefully you can adjust to the pace here in sleepy old Parksville." She looked at the floor. "Maybe finally find a nice girl to..."

"Mom!" He cut her off. "Oh my God. Enough with that!"

She surrendered. "Sorry, Jim. You're right. It's not my place." This was only a false retreat, though. In one breath she spat out, "But-I'm-just-saying-Lisah-is-still-in-town-and-I-know-you-two-were-always-so-close-growing-up."

Jim dropped the box he was holding and made for the door. "I'll be back with dinner." It took all his effort not to slam the door. He could hear his mother start to say something from inside as his feet hit the sidewalk. He headed for his favourite restaurant, not just in Parksville, but anywhere. As much as it pained him to come back to his home town with his tail between his legs, he was loving the idea of having Realm so close.

It was by far the most modern eatery in town, but it was casual and welcoming too. When he walked in, he could see that the menu took up the whole left wall. To the right was some future-retro seating, and at the back was the service counter, with a view to the kitchen. 

A thin, moustachioed guy in a tight beanie greeted Jim with a smile. "Hey, man, what'll it be?"

"Anything on special?"

"I'm glad you asked, my guy. We have two bowls on special right now. A roast chicken bowl with shredded carrot, avocado, roasted kale, and curry aioli, served on rice. And we also got a pulled pork bowl, with pickled ginger, caramelized red onion, and julienned apple, served on couscous with a creamy sriracha sauce."

Jim was in heaven. He got both. By now, he was feeling sorry for being so short with his mom. He walked back down the strip, with the water to his left. The town green space was between him and the ocean. He could vividly remember rolling down the big grassy hill with Lisah. For a few years' worth of summer holidays in high school they were inseparable. He sighed. His mother was right.

He had almost made it home when he stopped. Normally the best dressed person in town would have been someone in business casual, so when two tall men in black suits and black hats stepped out of a black Lincoln Town Car he had to do a double take. They were heading for the beach. *Why not park down there though? And what could they possibly be hoping to do?*

Jim chuckled to himself when he pictured the two of them with their ironed slacks rolled up and their jackets laying on thew sand as they splashed in the water. His tummy rumbled and he forgot the thought altogether.

At home, they ate and the tension was relieved. They upheld their tradition of tuning into Sly Johnny's Saturday evening broadcast, *Deep End of the High Tide.*
