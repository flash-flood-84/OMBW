# Chapter Four: The Hospital

Julie was recovering much quicker than Steve. Some locals had heard their screaming that night on the beach and rushed over to find them both unconscious. From there, they were whisked away to Nanaimo Regional General Hospital. Since they were a couple and their symptoms were the same, they managed to get a room together. Now, a couple of days later, Julie was able to get up and walk around. Steve was slowly getting better, but he still got a bit lightheaded if he wasn't laying down. In spite of that, the doctors were happy with his progress, although they weren't sure what would have caused both Julie and Steve to pass out like that.

It was mid-morning when she got up to get coffee from the hospital cafe. It all seemed so crazy to her. As far as she could remember, beyond medical procedures, she had never actually lost consciousness at any point in her life. Additionally, she had no recollection of what happened that night on the beach. All she had were vague sensations and feelings. There was a warm, all-encompassing, red light, a feeling of terror, followed by calm, and the next thing she knew, she was waking up in the hospital.

Since they were stuck up there, she had to call into work to let them know she would be off for a few days. The dynamics in her office were pretty toxic and she dreaded ever calling in sick. Her boss was so horrible about it. Something was different this time, though. As soon as the thought of work popped into her head, she was able to calmly pick up the phone and call in. No pacing around needed. No deep breaths before.

"Hey, Bob. How's it going?"

"Julie! Where the heck are you? It's 10:15. Things aren't getting done."

Casually, as if she hadn't heard him, Julie said, "Oh, nice, ya. Anyways, I won't be able to come in today. Not feeling well. I'll touch base with you tomorrow. Toodles."

Bob, flabbergasted, started a speech that he never got to finish. "Julie. No, this isn't acceptable. I need you here by..."

*CLICK*

Julie hung up and silenced her phone. She wouldn't realize until next week, when she was back in the office, that she no longer had any feelings, good or bad, about her boss. It was actually her co-workers who pointed it out. She didn't quite know what to make of that, but she knew she was happy.

Note to reader: This newfound confidence in an employee sort of sent Bob into a spiral. He became more erratic as Julie became more confident and he started to lose what little power he had. Eventually, his boss took notice and it became so bad that Bob was fired. The office at large rejoiced and, to make it even better, Julie was promoted to Bob's old position. Isn't that nice? End note.

As she walked back to their room with the coffees, she couldn't help but reflect on the last time they were in a medical setting. It was another screening for the dry patch on Steve's hand. Although she was no longer nervous about work, she still couldn't relax about the diagnosis. Cancer, but manageable for now. She meant to get a second opinion while in the hospital, just in case, but she needed a way to do it secretly, maybe while Steve was asleep.

Rounding the corner to the hallway where their room was, she was surprised to see the door closed. Her brow furrowed when she looked through the glass of the door and saw two men, dressed in black suits, sitting and talking with Steve. She could hear his slow, strained speech. She didn't like this. In his current condition, he wasn't really even able to chat for longer than a few minutes without strain, and it certainly looked like the two men were grilling him.

She didn't mean to be so dramatic, but she did have to kick open the door, as it was heavy and her hands were full with the coffees. Both men rose suddenly, as if ready for action, but when they saw Julie they relaxed. 

"What's going on in here? Who are you?" Julie asked forcefully. 

The taller of the two spoke in a professional and flat voice. "Sorry ma'am, and excuse us. We are just asking Mr. Brady a few questions here."

"Questions about what? Can't you tell he isn't able to talk right now?" She motioned to Steve, who had closed his eyes again, his head resting on the pillow.

"Again, sorry ma'am, but time is of the essence. We need to ask you about what you saw at the beach the other..." He stopped then as a nurse walked in. 

Angie, their nurse, who was very lovely and had become quite friendly with Julie over the last few days, now had a scowl on her face. "There are no visitors at this time of day. Who let you in here?"

At that, the two men in black nodded at each other and headed for the door, the tall one muttering, "Good day, ladies. Sorry for any inconvenience."

Angie had grabbed the headset off the wall phone and was just about to call security when Steve opened his eyes and said, in a groggy voice, "It's okay. They were nice." His head bobbled a bit. "They were just asking me about the Mothman." With a smile on his face, he passed out again.

Together, Angie and Julie said, "The... what?" but it was too late. Steve couldn't be roused. Angie checked his vitals, and, seeming satisfied, shrugged her shoulders. 

"I'm sorry, Julie, but we're so short today I need to get to my other patients. Let me know when I come by again if you'd like to file a security report or something. I might forget to bring it up though. Oh crap!" Something in her pocket started beeping. "See you soon! Gotta run." She quickly walked out the door. Julie could hear the soft squish of her orthotic nursing shoes receding into the hall.

Still a bit dazed, Julie remembered that she was hoping to ask someone about the dry patch on Steve's hand. She couldn't blame Angie for rushing out, but she was frustrated all the same. She often tried to avoid bringing attention to the dry patch, and wouldn't let Steve catch her looking at it, but since he was asleep she figured she could take a closer look.

She moved over to his left side. She was between him and the window. Morning light was streaming into the room. Seeing nothing and realizing it was the wrong hand, she rolled her eyes. *I can't even keep my right and left straight. Maybe I'm more affected by whatever happened than I thought.* His right arm was under the hospital blanket. Grabbing the top of the off-white fabric, she slowly pulled it down. *Okay now I'm really losing it. Nothing on this hand. Must have missed it on the left one.* She went back over to his left side and held up his hand to the window. Still nothing.

She stared in disbelief. Now she took both of his hands in hers and was alternately raising them to her eyes.

"Ughh. Julie. What time is it? I was just dreaming I was a marionette and my mother was the puppeteer. Isn't that funny?"

Before he opened his eyes, she dropped his hands and furtively looked out the window, biting her lip.

"Very funny, Steve." She was still confused, but a small smile crept onto her face and her eyes became misty. It seemed crazy, but could she allow herself to hope?
