The sun had just set, but its glow bloomed up through the watery horizon and into the sky. A light breeze came in from the ocean and put a hint of fall into the late August night. The tide was high, but there was still a comfortable boundary of soft sand between the water and the boardwalk. A few weeks ago, the couple would have had to jockey for space, but, with the shoulder season approaching, the bulk of the tourists had left the small beach town.

Their blanket was laden with a little feast. She had gone to the farmer's market that morning to grab cucumber, radish, and carrots, which she cut into perfect little spears and discs. That afternoon, she had also made hummus and tzatziki. He, naturally, had brought meat, cheese, bread, and red wine. For dessert, they had laughed when they bought it, but now it seemed perfect - on the drive up from Victoria to Parksville, they had stopped in Nanaimo and actually bought Nanaimo bars.

As they finished the meal, the last bit of light had faded away and the stars started to come out. The light breeze continued and a more noticeable chill came into the air. Too full from the meal for any funny business, they held each other under a muslin. Sitting, both facing the water, she was between his legs, resting her back on his chest, and he had  his arms draped loosely around her.

She eyed the rough, flaking patch of skin on his hand. *Everything will be fine,* she told herself. *The doctors say they can contain it at this stage.* She tried to ground herself by looking for the Big Dipper. After realizing it was the wrong time of year, she struck up a conversation. The talk drifted between quick reflections on their little vacation, the food, and their feelings about going back to work on Monday, until eventually it faded into comfortable silence.

The wind suddenly died.

He noticed she had become very still and was taking deep, slow breaths. He smiled. It was such a special feeling when she fell asleep in his arms. He knew they should head back to the hotel but found himself slowly slipping into a doze.

He tripped into waking, as you do when you're almost asleep. He couldn't believe it - the tiny peaks of sand in front of him were tipped with the red light of sunrise. He checked his watch. 

11:00 pm

"What?" he grunted.

She started and croaked, "Whadisit?" Clearing her throat, she tried again. "What is it, Steve?"

"Are you seeing this light, Julie?"

"You mean the brake lights?"

"Oh, honey, no. The nearest parking spot is twenty feet to the left of us."

"Don't 'Oh, honey' me!" She was half-joking. "You god damn mansplainer, thinking I, as a small-brained woman, have no visual-spatial awareness!"  

He let out a small chuckle. "Well, dear, we do have an easy way to settle this, but I admit, after the wine and food, I think standing up and turning around might be a team effort."

They detached from each other and worked to get solid footing in the soft sand. They rose and turned around.

He seemed to hear it in his head before it reached his ears - a piercing whistle. Looking at Julie, her face bathed in red light, he realized she was screaming at the top of her lungs. He turned to face the light, and it was as if the light itself struck him down. As he began to yell too, he could see more clearly now the source of the light. He began to slip from consciousness as everything was flooded and overwhelmed by the deep, irradiated red glow. And then all was quiet.
