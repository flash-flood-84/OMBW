# Chapter Three: The Radio Station

"Aaaaand that was *Private Eyes,* by Hall & Oates," the radio host said in a smooth, low voice. "Next up is *Fly by Night,* by Rush. But, before that..." He paused and triggered playback of ominous synth music. "...The sun has set on the beach. Night has fallen. And it is time..." He paused again, now playing a creaking door hinge sound. "...For *The Deep End of High Tide.*"

The music hit a crescendo before softly transitioning into smoother, yet still vaguely ominous backing tracks.

His radio station was really just a shed on his acreage in Errington. It was a shabby plot of land that was passed down through his family. It was a mix of rocky, dry earth, which was no good for farming, and old-growth trees that he didn't have the heart to cut down. His land was wild and that was fine. In spite of that, he had done a good job of making the station comfortable. It had a kitchenette and a tiny bathroom. The minimalist space had one big window, a faux persian rug, and a couple of tall lamps that threw warm, golden light.

In one corner, he had a surprisingly small, yet efficient, broadcasting setup. It was a heavy, wooden desk with a little mixing board, microphone, and an old iMac desktop computer. Wiring ran out from the shack, across the yard to the house, and up to a large antenna on the roof.

By pure geographic luck, there was a dead zone in the local FM band from 98 all the way to 105. The stations on those frequencies just couldn't reach into Parksville and the surrounding areas. The community council, responding to calls for more local radio stations, put out a posting for people to apply for airspace, as long as they broadcast the signal themselves. By further good luck, Johnny was the only applicant, and his weird little radio station was born. Little did the council know that it would be a mix of 70's and 80's hits, with a heavy dose of conspiracy theory coverage.

"Dear listeners," Johnny said in a hushed tone. "The weather is changing. Mother earth is not what she once was. Temperatures are rising. Animals are responding to this. Now, we know the American government is making use of underground labs to experiment on the human genome. This is well understood. We've all seen photos of the human-animal hybrids. Plain as day. You can ask anybody. But did you know that the combination of extreme energy consumption and dangerous chemical runoff from these facilities is one of, if not the largest, contributor to global warming?

He dug deeper into his low, smooth voice. "Well, deny it if you must, but one thing you can't deny is the animals' behaviour. Reports of all sorts of insects migrating north are rampant. Scientists have confirmed a northwestern migration to Vancouver Island of species indigenous to regions as far away as southern Appalachia."

A light on a small module next to the mixing board began to blink. Johnny's eyes widened. A caller! He begged his friends to call in and pretend to be conspiracy theorists to spice things up, but he didn't have anyone lined up for tonight. This was a real caller. He couldn't believe it.

"Hold it right there, folks." He tried to keep his voice level and calm, despite his excitement. "We have someone calling in."

Johnny tapped a few buttons on the module and patched in the caller. "Hello there, you're with Sly Johnny on The Deep End of High Tide. What's your name and where ya callin' from?"

An old, gruff voice began, "Johnny, hey ya. M'name's Will, but everyone calls me Big Billy."

An echo from Big Billy's line rang out. "BILLY BILLY BILLY..."

Johnny winced. "Will!"

"WILL WILL WILL..."

"You have to turn off the radio on your side!"

"SIDE SIDE SIDE..."

"There's an echo, Will!

The echo cut out. "Oh shit, sorry, Johnny. Oh crap. I didn't mean to swear on air. Sorry again, man."

Johnny had his face in his hands at this point. His first real caller and this is what he gets? He looked at the time. 9:15 PM. *This guy's probably wasted.*

"It's okay, Will. Sorry, Big Billy. Where are you calling from?"

Big Billy seemed to have pulled himself together. "Thanks, Johnny. Love your show. Yep. I'm calling in from Ladysmith."

*Hoo dang,* Johnny thought. *I knew that booster would do the trick.* He was always trying to increase his broadcast range.

"And do you have any stories, secrets, sightings, or scaries to speak of?" This was his usual line with callers.

"Oh ya, you bet," Billy grunted with excitement. "I was sitting down to a barbecue dinner on the back porch. It was smokies, ribs, chicken thighs, and barbecue baked beans."

Johnny rolled his eyes. "Uh-huh... Go on."

"Anyways, all of a sudden the lights went out, which is strange because I just changed the lightbulb for the porch light. It was a late dinner and I wasn't about to eat in the dark. My wife, Mabel, starts yellin' at me about the light, and I says to Mabel, I says, 'Off my case, woman! I just changed the dang light. You don't believe me then let's have a look right now!'"

A shrill voice came from the background in Big Billy's line. "Dang it, William. Don't talk about me like that to Johnny. He's on the radio! My sister will hear up in Coombs!"

Johnny's face was in his hands again. "Okay, Bill. Get to the spooky, please."

There was a muffled scratching on the line.

"Johnny?" It was Mabel's voice in the foreground now. "Johnny, dear, I'll tell the rest. So, we both stand up and walk to the porch light. Our eyes hadn't adjusted to the dark yet and we could see the light was flickering. Except it wasn't the lightbulb that was the problem! The darn thing was plastered with moths! Like you've never seen before. Had to be twenty or so, wrapped up tight against it. We tried to shoo them away but they wouldn't budge. Suddenly, we heard something in the bushes at the back of the yard. Keep in mind, these are thick, ten feet tall blackberry bushes. Anyways, when we turned to look, all we could see were two glowing red lights in the bushes. Bill said they looked just like two hot charcoal bricks from his barbecue, except they were the size of soccer balls."

Mabel was breathing heavily now, with a rising tension in her voice.

"Before we knew it, a shadow grew out from behind the red lights. All we could hear next was a terrible whooshing sound and the lights flew up into the trees, disappearing. And you know what Johnny?"

Johnny was genuinely on the edge of his seat now. His voice was a whisper and his mouth was dry. "What, Mabel?"

"When we turned back to look at the porch, the moths were gone."
