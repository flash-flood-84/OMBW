# Chapter Three: The Radio Station

NO PROOFREADING YET PROBABLY HAS TYPOS

"Aaaaand that was *Private Eyes,* by Hall and Oates," the radio host said in a smooth, low voice. "Next up is *Fly by Night,* by Rush. But, before that..." He paused and triggered playback on ominous synth music. "The sun has set on the beach. Night has fallen. And it is time..." He paused again, now triggering a creaking door hinge sound. "For *The Deep End of High Tide.*"

The music hit a crescendo before softly transitioning into smoother, yet still vaguely ominous backing tracks.

His radio station was really just a shed on his acreage in Errington. A shabby plot of land that was passed down through his family. It was a mix of rocky, dry earth, which was no good for farming, and old-growth trees that he didn't have the heart to cut down. His land was wild and that was fine. In spite of that, he had done a good job of making the station comfortable. It had a kitchenette and a tiny bathroom. The minimalist space had one big window, a faux persian rug, a couple tall, and two lamps that threw warm, golden light.

In one corner, he had a surprisingly small, yet efficient, broadcasting setup. It was a heavy, wooden desk with a little mixing board, microphone, and an old iMac desktop computer. Wiring ran out from the shack, across the yard to the house, and up to a large antenna on the roof.

By pure geographic luck, there was a dead zone in the local fm band from 98 all the way to 105. The community council, responding to calls for more local radio stations, put out a posting for people to apply for airspace, as long as they broadcasted the signal themselves. By further good luck, Johnny was the only applicant, and his weird little radio station was born. Little did the council know that it would be a mix of 70's and 80's hits, with a heavy dose of conspiracy theory coverage.

"Dear listeners," Johnny said in a hushed tone. "The weather is changing. Mother earth is not what she once was. Temperatures are rising. Animals are responding to this. Now, we know the American government is making use of underground labs to experiment on the human genome. This is well understood. We've all seen photos of the human-animal hybrids. Plain as day. You can ask anybody. But did you know that the combination of extreme energy consumption, and dangerous chemical runoff from these facilities is one of, if not the largest contributor to global warming?

He dug deeper into his low, smooth voice. "Well, deny it if you must, but one thing you can't deny is the animals' behaviour. Reports of all sorts of insects migrating north are rampant. Scientists have confirmed a north western migration to Vancouver Island from species indigenous to as far away as southern Appalachia."
