# Chapter Two: The Two Knights

Sir Jacob Weathers sat up in bed. He tried to sit up in bed. He swore, craned his neck, spat, and managed to lurch onto his elbows. Finally, he sat up in bed. The threadbare curtains of his little room at the inn let in too much light. He squinted, looking for the bottle of wine he had had a small cup of the night before. Finding it on the floor next to the bed, it appeared to be open and almost finished. He grabbed it and sloshed down what was left.

"Uhgh!" His voice was thin and gravelly. He cleared his throat and muttered to himself, "God damned Stilsons got me going on a god damned boat to the north. They'd better have good wine up there."

It was a month ago when, at court, the Stilsons had been officially elevated to the title of lord and lady. It had been a blustery October day, with dark gray clouds. The great hall had to be lit with torches even though it was noon. 

"I'll not hold court in the dark!" the young king hotly protested. "More torches! More candles!" he demanded.

Sir Jacob, who was placed by the rear entrance behind the throne, had to stifle a laugh. **If what he does here is considered holding court, then I was doing the same in my chamber pot this morning**.

The king had only recently fully claimed his power. The old king and queen passed away when he was ten and, up until now, King Billiam had been king only in title. The rule of the realm was left to his uncle Frederick, acting as steward of the throne. So far, Billiam was doing a shit job.

Sir Jacob watched the king making the piss-poor decision to give a castle to these fools - the Stilsons. Commoners, really. The disapproval from the courtiers was palpable. *I know how this plays out. A few more slip-ups like this and I'll be asked that the king has a tragic accident. Wouldn't be the first time.*

Jacob vaguely remembered something about Castle Gray originally being entrusted to the church, but he didn't really pay attention to details. He was a knight, but, in truth, he had no valor. All he valued was gold, wine, and wenches. Killing was good too, and all the better if he could kill for coin.

Jacob could sense that King Billiam was about to conclude whatever the hell he was going on about. The knight was smart enough to at least pay attention to the end bits of courtly proceedings.

Billiam continued in his thin voice, "...And for special valor and aid during the Silver Fork rebellion, Blake and Belinda Stilson shall be elevated to the title of Lord and Lady. Furthermore, they will be granted lordship over Castle Gray."

The Stilsons, both in ill-fitting and hastily acquired finery, stood tall and proud, although obviously uncomfortable in court.

"And to aid them in their journey north, I shall send Sir Jacob and Sir Milton."

Jacob's eyes shot wide open. He heard someone shout, "What the bloody hell?" When all the eyes of the court fell on him, he realized that it had been his own voice.

Now, leagues away from the castle in the Port of Sea-Lions, he was desperately in search of food and drink to clear his hangover before setting sail for the Port of Seals. On his ambling path through the docks he spotted Jimothy, his squire.

"Jimothy!" he shouted. The boy awkwardly lurched and craned his neck in Jacob's direction, almost falling over. "What the hell have you got there?" Jacob pointed to two large sacks.

The boy sheepishly replied, "Eels and clams, sir." He studied the ground, unable to meet Jacob's gaze.

The knight scowled. "Did Sir Milton put you up to this? I tasked you with ensuring the Windy Wench had a cask of wine for me on board." He quickly scanned up and down Jimothy. "The coin purse! Where is the coin I gave you for the wine?"

"Sir. I. Sir," the boy stammered.

"Out with it, boy! If you spent my gold on eels and clams, so help me I'll..."

"Sir, I'm sorry, sir. It was a widower and a mother who had lost all her children."

Jacob rolled his eyes. This would be easy enough, but he would still need to find some way to punish his helpless squire.

"Take me to them," he growled.

The two made their way down the waterside road. A cool wind blew in from the water. Ships of all sorts bobbed by the docks. Jimothy stepped lightly, as if afraid to make a sound and further anger Jacob. Jacob plodded heavily along, weighed down by his chain mail, sword, and his age. When they reached the two vendors, Jacob gripped his sword. "Them?" he asked the boy grimly.

Jimothy studied his boots, nodding slightly, still not able to look Jacob in the eyes.

"Don't worry, boy. I won't hurt them too bad."

"NO!" Jimothy blurted out. "Leave them be, sir. Please!"

This outburst took Jacob by surprise. Jimothy barely spoke, and when he did it was often in snivelling mumbles. "Whatever I do to them, boy, it will be a lot better than what I'll do to you and everyone else on that boat if I'm three days with no wine." Jacob shot a glance at Grafilda. "You there! Fish wench. Give this boy his coin back and you'll be lucky if you get back what you sold him!"

"I'm a clam wench actually m'lord and a sale is a sale. The deal is done. That's how it is in the port."

As if there was an echo, Davey murmured while nodding, "The deal is done. That's how it is."

It was then that Jacob popped his sword out of its hilt by an inch and looked down at it. He looked back up at Grafilda, grinning and revealing a gap toothed smile below his mustache. The light of the morning sun flashed over the blade.

Grafilda gasped and stumbled backwards. Davey looked as if he was deciding whether to run or stand his ground. Jacob was drawing a breath to utter a threat when he heard a familiar voice.

"Jacob! Jimothy!" It was a deep and jovial sound. "How good of you to support the local seafood economy." The tall, middle aged and fair faced knight casually strode into the space between Jacob and the merchants. He smiled at Jimothy. "Thank you my good squire. Those clams and eels will be perfect for our journey north. And Jacob. What seems to be your bother?" He turned and winked at Grafila, which caused her to stumble yet again, this time with flushed cheeks.

Jacob began in his thin rasp, "The boy spent the money I gave him for wine on fish!"

Grafilda let out a barely perceptible whisper. "Clams, actually."

"Eels ain't fish neither, I don't fink," chimed in Davey.

"Shut up and give me my coin!" Jacob advanced on them, slowly drawing his sword further out of its hilt.

Milton laughed heartily. "Alright, alright. That's enough Jacob. How about this? I will buy the lovely seafood from you." He brought out his own heavy coin purse and handed Jacob a gold coin and a few bronze ones. Jacob muttered something under his breath and stormed off, but not before giving them all a deathly stare. *Fucking commoners. Fucking Milton. Who knows. Maybe he'll have his own little accident on our way up north.*

Milton looked at Jimothy then. In a friendly voice he asked, "What is the lesson here, my young squire?"

Before he could answer, Davey blurted, "Your squire? I thought ee was the other's? A squire can't 'ave two knights can'ee?"

Jimothy blushed, as if from shame.

"Strange, I know, my fellow." He clapped the boy on the shoulder. "But Jimothy here is more than able."

Jimothy, now with a smile on his face, asked, "What is the lesson, sir?"

Milton smiled. "Never get between a drunk and his drink."
